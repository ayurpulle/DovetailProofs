\title{Congruence Obstructions in Exact Predictive Agents}
\author{Ayur Pulle\\ \small{Dovetail Research}}
\date{\small August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle


\begin{abstract}
Every exact predictor of a stochastic process contains, as a quotient
of its update dynamics, the process's canonical predictive machine.
We prove this at the level of transition monoids: a descent lemma
converts the state level refinement into a
surjection of monoids, so the canonical congruence lattice appears as
an interval inside every exact predictor's lattice of admissible
lumpings. Below the predictive
machine sits a possibility machine recording only which futures can
occur, contributing a nested outer interval, above it, the minimal
linear predictor embeds into an extension of the canonical monoid by
transition likelihoods, and passing back to the canonical monoid is
exactly the projection that discards the likelihood coordinate.  A word by word
companion bounds forced memory, after each observed word, every exact
predictor retains at least as many distinctions as the canonical
machine. The layers separate.  Generic first order
Markov chains carry a non distributive $M_3$ interval although a
last symbol predictor suffices, with every probability value sitting
in the likelihood extension.  For two standard example processes we
compute exactly that the entire canonical monoid is determined by
the pattern of possible words alone.  Non distributivity of the inherited interval is
therefore generic and certifies nothing about internal
representations, which obstructions genuinely constrain decomposition
remains open.
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:positioning}

Let a world produce a sequence of observations from a finite alphabet
$\Obs$.  An exact predictive agent has enough information to return
the conditional distribution of every finite future, it may
nevertheless have redundant states or implementation specificities.
Our question is: which predictive structure, the canonical
predictive states together with their observation labelled updates,
must every such agent contain, or realise as a quotient?

The answer is a tower of quotient theorems together with one
quantitative bound: every reachable exact predictor maps onto the
process's canonical predictive machine, which maps onto a coarser
possibility machine recording which futures can occur, the minimal
linear predictor embeds into a likelihood extension of the canonical
monoid, and the process fixes a word by word lower bound on the
distinctions any exact predictor must retain
(Sections~\ref{sec:agent}--\ref{sec:quantitative}, \ref{sec:rank}).
Each quotient plants an interval of admissible lumpings inside the
predictor's congruence lattice, we call the non distributive
configurations these intervals can carry \emph{obstructions}: no
reimplementation can remove them, and they provably obstruct exactly
one thing, a union/intersection coordinate representation of the
lattice of admissible lumpings (Section~\ref{sec:coordinates}).
Whether they obstruct any decomposition of the agent itself is left
open.  The interval reads exactly the middle layer of the tower,
blind to likelihoods by construction.
All of this concerns prediction, not control, Section
\ref{sec:controllers} shows fixed-task optimality is insufficient and
states the assumptions under which a controller inherits the same
structure.  No claim is made that a neural network's raw hidden state
is automatically a linear realisation, or that any of this locates
features in activations.

The state level foundations are classical: identification of histories
by their conditional future laws, with any equally predictive rival
refining that partition
\cite{CrutchfieldYoung1989,ShaliziCrutchfield2001}, the
$\varepsilon$-transducer for action/output processes
\cite{BarnettCrutchfield2015}, and Hankel minimisation
with its quotient onto the minimal realisation
\cite{Fliess1974,Jaeger2000}.  Two lines are engaged rather
than built on: behavioural recovery of an environment from
goal conditioned policies
\cite{Richens2025,Cifuentes2026,LuEtAl2026}, taken up conditionally in
Section~\ref{sec:controllers}, and the empirical study of predictive
structure in trained networks \cite{ShaiEtAl2024}, whose example
processes are computed exactly in
Section~\ref{subsec:computed-support}.

The contribution is the transition monoid layer above these results:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item a single descent lemma that converts any update commuting
        surjection of state machines
        into a monoid surjection and an inherited congruence interval
        (Lemma~\ref{lem:descent}),
  \item the tower: nested inherited intervals recording possibility
        and prediction, and an embedding of the minimal linear
        predictor into a likelihood extension of the canonical monoid
        whose second coordinate projection is the congruence
        invariant (Sections~\ref{sec:support}, \ref{sec:quantitative}),
  \item a word by word memory bound: the live rank profile of the process
        lower bounds the distinctions any exact predictor retains
        after each word, with total forgetting and unbounded memory
        both realised by explicit processes (Section~\ref{sec:rank}),
  \item exact computations locating the congruence structure of two
        standard example processes entirely at the possibility layer,
        and of generic Markov chains entirely at the likelihood layer
        (Sections~\ref{sec:support}, \ref{sec:reset}),
  \item a scope limit: non distributivity of the inherited interval is
        generic and is not a certificate of entangled features
        (Sections~\ref{sec:reset}, \ref{sec:coordinates}).
\end{enumerate}
All code supporting the computations, exact and reproducible, is
available in the paper's repository \cite{DovetailRepo}.

\section{Stochastic languages and conditional futures}
\label{sec:process}

\begin{definition}[Stochastic language]
A stochastic language over a finite alphabet $\Obs$ is a function
\[
  p:\Obs^*\longrightarrow[0,1]
\]
such that
\[
  p(\eps)=1,
  \qquad
  p(u)=\sum_{o\in\Obs}p(uo)
  \quad (u\in\Obs^*).
\]
Here $p(u)$ is the probability that the process begins with the word $u$,
the second condition is simple consistency, that the summed probabilities of all one symbol extensions to $u$ are
equal to the probability of $u$.
\end{definition}

If the process is also stationary,
then its probabilities additionally satisfy
\[
  p(u)=\sum_{o\in\Obs}p(ou).
\]

Stationarity may be imposed, but none of the quotient arguments uses it.

For a history $u$ with $p(u)>0$, define the conditional future
\[
  q_u(v)=\frac{p(uv)}{p(u)}
  \qquad(v\in\Obs^*).
\]
Then $q_u(\eps)=1$ and $q_u(v)=\sum_o q_u(vo)$.  Histories $u,u'$ are
predictively equivalent when $q_u=q_{u'}$.

If $p(u)=0$, consistency and non negativity imply $p(uv)=0$ for every
$v$, and then conditional prediction after $u$ is undefined.  Represent
every such impossible continuation by one symbol
$\dagger$.

\begin{definition}[Canonical predictive states]
The predictive state set of $p$ is
\[
  X_p=\{q_u:p(u)>0\}.
\]
Define $\widehat X_p=X_p\cup\{\dagger\}$.
\end{definition}

This is the usual predictive/causal state identification by complete future
law \cite{CrutchfieldYoung1989,ShaliziCrutchfield2001}, with one
deviation to flag: we condition on finite histories, so $X_p$ can
contain transient states, where computational mechanics classes almost
every infinite past.  The relation between the two presentations, and
their generator counterpart, is settled in the finite-state ergodic
case in \cite{TraversCrutchfield2025}.

For $o\in\Obs$, define $\tau_o:\widehat X_p\to\widehat X_p$ by
\[
  \tau_o(q_u)=
  \begin{cases}
    q_{uo},&p(uo)>0,\\
    \dagger,&p(uo)=0,
  \end{cases}
  \qquad
  \tau_o(\dagger)=\dagger.
\]

\begin{lemma}
The map $\tau_o$ is well-defined: if $q_u=q_{u'}$, then either both
$p(uo)$ and $p(u'o)$ vanish, or $q_{uo}=q_{u'o}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The one symbol probabilities agree:
\[
  \frac{p(uo)}{p(u)}=q_u(o)=q_{u'}(o)
  =\frac{p(u'o)}{p(u')}.
\]
Thus one is zero exactly when the other is.  If they are positive, then for every
$v$,
\[
  q_{uo}(v)=\frac{q_u(ov)}{q_u(o)}
  =\frac{q_{u'}(ov)}{q_{u'}(o)}
  =q_{u'o}(v).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
Let
\[
  S_p=\langle\tau_o:o\in\Obs\rangle
  \subseteq\mathcal T_{\widehat X_p}
\]
be the transformation monoid generated by the predictive updates, inside
$\mathcal T_{\widehat X_p}$, the monoid of all maps from $\widehat X_p$ to
itself under composition.  Composition is
ordinary function composition.  For a word $w=o_1\cdots o_n$, write
$\tau_w=\tau_{o_n}\circ\cdots\circ\tau_{o_1}$, so that
$\tau_w(q_u)=q_{uw}$ whenever $p(uw)>0$.
\end{definition}

For later use, write
\[
  L_p=\{w\in\Obs^*:p(w)>0\}
\]
for the set of possible words.  Consistency gives
$p(u)\ge p(uv)$, so $L_p$ is closed under prefixes, and
$p(u)=\sum_o p(uo)$ shows every possible word has at least one possible
one symbol extension.

\section{Every exact predictive agent contains the canonical machine}
\label{sec:agent}

The results of this paper repeatedly use one mechanism, so we
state it here.

\begin{lemma}[Descent]
\label{lem:descent}
Let $Q$ and $Y$ be sets, each equipped with a family of maps
$\delta_o:Q\to Q$ and $\tau_o:Y\to Y$ indexed by the same finite alphabet
$\Obs$, and let $L:Q\to Y$ be a surjection satisfying
\[
  L\circ\delta_o=\tau_o\circ L
  \qquad(o\in\Obs).
\]
Write $S_Q=\langle\delta_o\rangle$ and $S_Y=\langle\tau_o\rangle$ for the
generated transformation monoids, with the composition convention
$\delta_w=\delta_{o_n}\circ\cdots\circ\delta_{o_1}$ for
$w=o_1\cdots o_n$, and likewise for $\tau_w$.  Then
\[
  \Phi(\delta_w)=\tau_w
\]
is a well-defined surjective monoid homomorphism $\Phi:S_Q\to S_Y$, and,
with $\kappa=\ker\Phi$,
\[
  \Con(S_Y)\cong[\kappa,\top]\subseteq\Con(S_Q).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Iterating the displayed equations gives $L\circ\delta_w=\tau_w\circ L$ for
every word $w$.  If $\delta_w=\delta_v$ then
$\tau_w\circ L=\tau_v\circ L$, and surjectivity of $L$ forces
$\tau_w=\tau_v$, so $\Phi$ is well-defined.  For words $v,w$ the convention
gives $\delta_w\circ\delta_v=\delta_{vw}$ and
$\tau_w\circ\tau_v=\tau_{vw}$, so $\Phi$ respects composition, and it hits
every generator $\tau_o$, so it is surjective.  The interval statement is
the congruence correspondence theorem for the surjection $\Phi$
\cite{Howie1995}.
\end{proof}

Congruences and their lattice are used throughout, we fix the
vocabulary here.  A congruence on a monoid $S$ is an equivalence relation
$\sim$ compatible with multiplication on both sides: $s\sim t$ implies
$asb\sim atb$ for all $a,b\in S$.  


Compatibility is what makes
congruences the admissible lumpings of dynamics: it says that
substituting indistinguishable transformations into any past and future
dynamical context cannot make them distinguishable, so once
admissibility is defined as a lumping of transition operators, the
admissible hierarchy is exactly $\Con(S)$.\footnote{This is a
conditional statement: it does not make every algebraic congruence
meaningful for a particular agent's dynamics.}  This concerns
transformations, not predictive states, coarse graining states is a
different construction.

$\Con(S)$ denotes the set of all congruences on $S$, ordered by
refinement, the intersection of two congruences is a congruence, and any
family of congruences has a coarsest common refinement, so $\Con(S)$ is a
lattice, with bottom the equality relation and top the relation merging
everything.  The interval $[\kappa,\top]$ consists of the congruences at
least as coarse as $\kappa$, and the correspondence theorem says these are
in order-preserving bijection with the congruences of the quotient
$S/\kappa$.  Finally, $M_3$ and $N_5$ are the two five-element lattices shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:m3n5}, whose presence as a sublattice is equivalent to
the failure of distributivity (Section~\ref{sec:coordinates}).


No structure on $Q$ or $Y$ is assumed: no dead state, no linearity, no
probabilistic interpretation.  Everything specific to prediction enters
through the construction of a particular surjection $L$.  One
convention remark for later use: a congruence is compatible with
multiplication on both sides, so a monoid and its opposite (the same
elements multiplied in reverse order) have the same congruences,
$\Con(S)=\Con(S^{\mathrm{op}})$, composition-order conventions
therefore never affect congruence lattices.


\begin{definition}
A reachable exact predictive agent for $p$ consists of
\[
  A=(Q,q_0,d,\{\delta_o\}_{o\in\Obs},F),
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $Q$ is the set of agent states,
  \item $q_0\in Q$ is the initial state,
  \item $d\in Q$ is the dead state, the agent's version of
        $\dagger$, used for impossible continuations, like 
        $\dagger$, a single formal element adjoined to the live states,
  \item each $\delta_o:Q\to Q$ is the state update on observing $o$,
  \item $F$ reads out the complete conditional future law
        represented by a live state, its domain $Q^+$ is
        defined below.
\end{itemize}


For $u=o_1\cdots o_n$, write
\[
  \delta_u=\delta_{o_n}\circ\cdots\circ\delta_{o_1},
  \qquad \delta_\eps=\id.
\]
The possible reachable states are
\[
  Q^+=\{\delta_u(q_0):p(u)>0\},
\]
the dead state $d$ is not in $Q^+$, and
$Q=Q^+\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{d\}$.  Each $\delta_o:Q\to Q$ satisfies
$\delta_o(d)=d$ and
\[
  p(u)>0,\ p(uo)=0
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \delta_o(\delta_u(q_0))=d.
\]
Finally, $F:Q^+\to X_p$ returns the state's complete conditional future law.
The predictor is exact when, for every $u$ with $p(u)>0$,
\[
  F(\delta_u(q_0))=q_u.
\]
\end{definition}

After an impossible extension there is no conditional future law, 
so exactness constrains nothing, rather than leave the update maps undefined, we route every impossible extension to the 
single dead state and keep it there. 
We call this the trimming convention, all dead branches are sent to one point, 
so the update maps are total and any two exact predictors can differ only on states that matter.


\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:predictor-quotient}
Let $A$ be a reachable exact predictive agent for $p$.  There is a surjection
\[
  L:Q\rightarrow\widehat X_p
\]
such that
\[
  L\circ\delta_o=\tau_o\circ L
  \qquad(o\in\Obs).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a non-dead reachable state $q=\delta_u(q_0)$, set $L(q)=q_u$, and send the
agent's dead state to $\dagger$.  This is well-defined: if
$\delta_u(q_0)=\delta_v(q_0)$, exactness gives
$q_u=F(\delta_u(q_0))=F(\delta_v(q_0))=q_v$.

Every element $q_u\in X_p$ is hit by the state $\delta_u(q_0)$, so $L$ is
surjective.  If $p(uo)>0$, then
\[
  L(\delta_o\delta_u(q_0))=q_{uo}
  =\tau_o(q_u)=\tau_oL(\delta_u(q_0)).
\]
If $p(uo)=0$, both sides are $\dagger$ by the trimming convention.  The equality
also holds at the absorbing dead state.
\end{proof}

This is an analogue of
the Refinement Lemma from computational mechanics, any equally predictive rival partition refines the
causal state partition, up to null histories \cite{ShaliziCrutchfield2001}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:descent} lifts that state-level refinement to transition
monoids.

Let $S_A=\langle\delta_o:o\in\Obs\rangle\subseteq\mathcal T_Q$.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:con-inheritance}
Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:descent} to the surjection of
Theorem~\ref{thm:predictor-quotient} yields a surjective monoid
homomorphism
\[
  \Phi:S_A\rightarrow S_p,
  \qquad
  \Phi(\delta_w)=\tau_w,
\]
and, with $\kappa=\ker\Phi$,
\[
  \Con(S_p)\cong[\kappa,\top]\subseteq\Con(S_A).
\]
In particular, if $\Con(S_p)$ is non-distributive then so is
$\Con(S_A)$: every $M_3$ or $N_5$ sublattice of $\Con(S_p)$ occurs
inside the interval $[\kappa,\top]$, since an interval is closed under
meets and joins.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Lemma~\ref{lem:descent}.
\end{proof}

This is the result for agents, different exact predictors may add
implementation distinctions below $\kappa$, but cannot remove the predictive interval above it.

\section{The possibility layer}
\label{sec:support}

The predictive state $q_u$ records how likely each future is.  A coarser
record keeps only which futures are possible at all, we call this
bare possibility.  This section shows
that this coarser structure is itself a canonical machine, sitting below
the predictive one, and that every exact predictor therefore inherits a
second, nested congruence interval.

\begin{definition}[Follower sets]
For $p(u)>0$, the follower set of $u$ is the set of futures it leaves
possible,
\[
  F_u=\{v\in\Obs^*:p(uv)>0\}.
\]
The support states of $p$ are
\[
  X_{\mathrm{supp}}=\{F_u:p(u)>0\},
  \qquad
  \widehat X_{\mathrm{supp}}
  =X_{\mathrm{supp}}\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{\dagger\}.
\]
\end{definition}

Each $F_u$ contains $\eps$ and is closed under prefixes.  The update on
follower sets is the same one-symbol shift as before:
\[
  \sigma_o(F)=
  \begin{cases}
    \{v:ov\in F\},&o\in F,\\
    \dagger,&o\notin F,
  \end{cases}
  \qquad
  \sigma_o(\dagger)=\dagger.
\]
Unlike the predictive update, no well-definedness check is needed: the
right-hand side is a function of the set $F$ itself, and
$\sigma_o(F_u)=F_{uo}$ whenever $p(uo)>0$, while $o\notin F_u$ exactly
when $p(uo)=0$.  Let
\[
  S_{\mathrm{supp}}
  =\langle\sigma_o:o\in\Obs\rangle
  \subseteq\mathcal T_{\widehat X_{\mathrm{supp}}},
\]
with the same composition convention as $S_p$.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:support-quotient}
The map
\[
  \sigma:\widehat X_p\to\widehat X_{\mathrm{supp}},
  \qquad
  \sigma(q_u)=F_u,
  \qquad
  \sigma(\dagger)=\dagger,
\]
is a well-defined surjection satisfying
$\sigma\circ\tau_o=\sigma_o\circ\sigma$ for every $o\in\Obs$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$F_u=\{v:q_u(v)>0\}$ is determined by the function $q_u$ alone, so
$\sigma$ is well-defined, and it is surjective because every follower set
arises from some possible $u$.  For the displayed equation at a live
state: if $p(uo)>0$ both sides equal $F_{uo}$, and if $p(uo)=0$ then
$q_u(o)=0$, so $o\notin F_u$ and both sides equal $\dagger$.  At
$\dagger$ both sides are $\dagger$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:support-tower}
Lemma~\ref{lem:descent} applied to $\sigma$ gives a surjective monoid
homomorphism $S_p\to S_{\mathrm{supp}}$ with kernel
$\kappa_{\mathrm{supp}}$, so
\[
  \Con(S_{\mathrm{supp}})
  \cong[\kappa_{\mathrm{supp}},\top]\subseteq\Con(S_p).
\]
Composing with Theorem~\ref{thm:predictor-quotient}, every reachable
exact predictive agent $A$ carries a nested pair of intervals
\[
  \Con(S_{\mathrm{supp}})
  \cong[\kappa',\top]
  \;\subseteq\;
  [\kappa,\top]
  \cong\Con(S_p)
  \;\subseteq\;
  \Con(S_A),
\]
where $\kappa'\supseteq\kappa$ is the kernel of the composite
$S_A\to S_p\to S_{\mathrm{supp}}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The composite of the two equivariant surjections
$Q\to\widehat X_p\to\widehat X_{\mathrm{supp}}$ (equivariant meaning
commuting with every update map) is again an equivariant surjection, so Lemma~\ref{lem:descent} applies to each arrow and to the
composite, the kernels nest because the composite identifies at least as
much as the first factor.
\end{proof}

Every exact predictor therefore inherits two layers of forced structure:
an outer interval recording distinctions of possibility, and inside it a
finer interval recording distinctions of conditional expectation.  The
outer layer is purely combinatorial: it depends only on the set $L_p$,
not on any probability value.\footnote{In the terminology of symbolic
dynamics, $\widehat X_{\mathrm{supp}}$ with the updates $\sigma_o$ is
the follower-set automaton of the language $L_p$, for stationary
processes whose support is a sofic shift this is a standard object
\cite{LindMarcus1995}, and the quotient $\sigma$ is the finite-history
analogue of the relation between the history $\varepsilon$-machine and
the Krieger cover \cite{TraversCrutchfield2025}.  We use none of that
theory beyond the name:
the layer enters only as a second instance of Lemma~\ref{lem:descent},
and no logical or language-theoretic classification of $L_p$ is
claimed or needed.}



\begin{remark}[When the layer is trivial]
\label{rem:full-support}
$S_{\mathrm{supp}}$ is the trivial monoid exactly when every finite word
is possible.  If $p(w)>0$ for all $w$, then $F_u=\Obs^*$ for every $u$,
there is a single support state, and every $\sigma_o$ is the identity.
Conversely, if some word is impossible, take a shortest impossible $wo$
with $p(w)>0$, then $\sigma_o$ sends $F_w$ to $\dagger$ and is not the
identity.  In particular, the full-support Markov chains of
Section~\ref{sec:reset} have a trivial possibility layer: their entire
canonical structure, including the $M_3$ interval, lives in the
predictive layer.  A process can thus force rich structure at one layer
and none at the other.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Two computed examples: the bottom layer can be everything}
\label{subsec:computed-support}

The nested intervals of Corollary~\ref{cor:support-tower} can degenerate
in either direction.  Remark~\ref{rem:full-support} gave one extreme,
the opposite occurs in two small hidden-Markov processes used as
standard examples in the interpretability literature
\cite{ShaiEtAl2024}.  The first, Z1R, cycles deterministically through
``0, then 1, then a fair coin''.  The second, RRXOR, emits two fair
coins and then their exclusive-or, repeatedly.  Both have impossible
words, so neither has a trivial possibility layer.

For each process we computed, in exact rational arithmetic from the
stationary initial condition, the predictive states, the canonical
monoid, its full congruence lattice, and the support
quotient, scripts and outputs are in the repository
\cite{DovetailRepo}.  For Z1R: $|X_p|=7$, $|S_p|=14$, $|\Con(S_p)|=8$, with
exactly one $M_3$ sublattice.  For RRXOR: $|X_p|=36$, $|S_p|=73$,
$|\Con(S_p)|=29$, again with exactly one $M_3$.  In both processes the
state map $\sigma$ is genuinely lossy: Z1R merges one pair of
predictive states, and RRXOR merges its $36$ live states into $20$
follower-set classes.  Yet in both, the monoid map
$S_p\to S_{\mathrm{supp}}$ is an \emph{isomorphism}:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{supp}}$ is the equality relation.  Words that act
identically on bare possibility already act identically on prediction.

Two consequences.  First, states and transformations separate cleanly:
coarse-graining the states loses information, while the induced lumping
of the transformations loses none, a concrete instance of the
distinction drawn in the congruence primer of Section~\ref{sec:agent},
exhibited inside a single
process.  Second, every congruence of $S_p$, including the $M_3$
witness, the concrete sublattice exhibiting non-distributivity, is an
invariant of the support language $L_p$ alone: any
process with the same impossibility pattern and arbitrary other
probabilities carries the same witness.  For these processes, whatever
the congruence interval detects, it detects without consulting a single
probability value.

Together with Remark~\ref{rem:full-support}, this exhibits both poles.
The full-support Markov chains of Section~\ref{sec:reset} have
$\kappa_{\mathrm{supp}}=\top$, and their $M_3$ contains
$\kappa_{\mathrm{supp}}$ in none of its five elements except the top:
that witness is built entirely from probabilities.  Z1R and RRXOR have
$\kappa_{\mathrm{supp}}=\bot$, and their witnesses lie at the
possibility layer in the strongest sense.  Because $\kappa_{\mathrm{supp}}$ is trivial in both processes, descent
holds here for every congruence, not only the witnesses, these examples
therefore do not test whether impossibility-built witnesses descend in
general.  A process with $\bot\subsetneq\kappa_{\mathrm{supp}}
\subsetneq\top$ would, and we do not have one.

\section{The finite-rank linear realisation}
\label{sec:linear}

The preceding sections used no linear structure.  This section builds
the canonical \emph{linear} predictor of $p$: a vector space carrying
one operator per symbol, whose word products reproduce every
probability.  Its operator monoid is the object whose comparison with
$S_p$, in Section~\ref{sec:quantitative}, isolates the likelihood
layer.  The construction is classical when $p$ has finite Hankel rank
\cite{Fliess1974,Jaeger2000}.

Let
\[
  W=\R^{\Obs^*}
\]
be the vector space of all real functions on finite futures, we never
choose a basis for $W$.  Define the unnormalised row
\[
  r_u(v)=p(uv),
\]
the $u$-th row of the infinite matrix $H_{u,v}=p(uv)$, classically
called the Hankel matrix of $p$ because its entries depend only on the
concatenation $uv$.  Let
\[
  V=\spn\{r_u:u\in\Obs^*\}\subseteq W,
\]
and define
\[
  \rank H:=\dim\spn\{r_u:u\in\Obs^*\}.
\]
Assume in this section that $\rank H<\infty$.  Then $\dim V=\rank H$.

For $o\in\Obs$, define
\[
  (T_of)(v)=f(ov).
\]
Then $T_or_u=r_{uo}$, so $T_o$ restricts to a linear endomorphism of
$V$.  Use a column convention.  For $w=o_1\cdots o_n$, put
\[
  T_w=T_{o_n}\cdots T_{o_1},
\]
then $T_wr_u=r_{uw}$.  Hence $w\mapsto T_w$ is a homomorphism into the
opposite monoid of $\End(V)$, this has no effect on congruence
lattices, as noted after Lemma~\ref{lem:descent}.  The readout is the
linear functional
\[
  \lambda:V\to\R,\qquad\lambda(f)=f(\eps),
\]
and
\[
  p(w)=\lambda(T_wr_\eps).
\]

The rows $r_u$ are not conditional predictive states: for $p(u)>0$,
\[
  r_u=p(u)q_u,
\]
a future law scaled by the probability of its history.  Two histories
with the same future law therefore give proportional rows, with a
positive ratio.  Call
\[
  [f]=\{cf:c>0\}
\]
the \emph{positive ray} through a nonzero $f\in V$, positivity matters,
since recovering $q_u$ from $r_u$ means normalising the
$\eps$-coordinate to one, which only a positive multiple permits.  The
assignment
\[
  \beta(q_u)=[r_u]
\]
is then a well-defined bijection from $X_p$ onto the set of rays of
nonzero rows, and $\beta$ intertwines the conditional and projective
updates:
\[
  \beta(\tau_o(q_u))=T_o\cdot\beta(q_u)
\]
whenever $p(uo)>0$, with impossible extensions sent to $\dagger$ on
both sides.  We henceforth identify $X_p$ with this ray set.

\begin{proposition}
The realisation $(V,r_\eps,\{T_o\},\lambda)$ is reachable and observable, and
therefore minimal.  Every reachable linear realisation of $p$ has dimension at
least $\dim V$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Reachability is the definition $V=\spn\{T_ur_\eps:u\in\Obs^*\}$.  If
$f\in V$ satisfies $\lambda(T_vf)=0$ for every $v$, then
$f(v)=0$ for every future $v$, so $f=0$, this is observability.  The
surjection constructed in Proposition~\ref{prop:linear-quotient} below gives
the dimension bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:linear-quotient}
Let $V'$ be a vector space, $x_0\in V'$, $A_o\in\End(V')$, and
$\lambda'\in(V')^*$.  For $w=o_1\cdots o_n$, set
\[
  A_w=A_{o_n}\cdots A_{o_1}.
\]
Suppose these data are a reachable linear realisation of the same stochastic
language:
\[
  p(w)=\lambda'(A_wx_0)
  \quad(w\in\Obs^*),
  \qquad
  V'=\spn\{A_ux_0:u\in\Obs^*\}.
\]
Then there is a surjective linear map $L:V'\rightarrow V$ satisfying
\[
  LA_o=T_oL
  \qquad(o\in\Obs).
\]
It therefore induces a surjective homomorphism from the agent's operator monoid
onto the minimal operator monoid.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $x\in V'$, define its future behaviour by
\[
  (Lx)(v)=\lambda'(A_vx).
\]
Reachability writes every $x$ as a linear combination of states $A_ux_0$, and
\[
  L(A_ux_0)=r_u.
\]
Thus $Lx\in V$ and $L$ is onto.  Moreover,
\[
  (LA_ox)(v)=\lambda'(A_vA_ox)
  =(T_oLx)(v),
\]
so $LA_o=T_oL$.  Since linear surjections are in particular surjections,
Lemma~\ref{lem:descent} applies to $L$ and gives the induced monoid map
\[
  \Psi:\langle A_o:o\in\Obs\rangle\rightarrow
  \langle T_o:o\in\Obs\rangle,
  \qquad
  \Psi(A_w)=T_w.
\]
\end{proof}

The result applies to exact weighted automata, observable-operator
models, and other reachable linear predictors of the complete joint
language.

\section{Conditional dynamics vs quantitative prediction}
\label{sec:quantitative}

Let $S_{\mathrm{lin}}=\langle T_o:o\in\Obs\rangle\subseteq\End(V)$.  Its action
on the positive Hankel rays is
\[
  T\cdot[r_u]=
  \begin{cases}
    [Tr_u],&Tr_u\ne0,\\
    \dagger,&Tr_u=0,
  \end{cases}
  \qquad T\cdot\dagger=\dagger.
\]
This gives a monoid homomorphism
\[
  \alpha:S_{\mathrm{lin}}\longrightarrow
  \mathcal T_{\widehat X_p}.
\]
Its image is the canonical predictive transition monoid $S_p$ and
\[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{pred}}=\ker\alpha
\]
identifies operators that induce the same conditional state update, including
the same pattern of impossible continuations.

There is a simple computable lower bound.  Define
\[
  T\mathrel{\theta_{\mathrm{scal}}}T'
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  T'=cT\text{ for some }c>0.
\]
This is a congruence on $S_{\mathrm{lin}}$: positive scalar multiplication is
compatible with composition on both sides, and
\[
  \theta_{\mathrm{scal}}\subseteq\kappa_{\mathrm{pred}}.
\]
The inclusion is strict in general: agreement on every ray fixes a
scalar for each ray separately, and nothing forces these to coincide.
Section~\ref{sec:reset} exhibits a process-realisable strict
inclusion.

The quotient by $\kappa_{\mathrm{pred}}$ can forget quantitative
information.  For $x=[r_u]\in X_p$, define the likelihood cocycle
(cocycle meaning only that it composes by the chain rule displayed below)
\[
  \ell_T(x)=\frac{\lambda(Tr_u)}{\lambda(r_u)},
\]
with value $0$ when $Tr_u=0$, and set $\ell_T(\dagger)=1$.  This is independent
of the positive representative of the ray and satisfies
\[
  \ell_{TU}(x)=\ell_T(U\cdot x)\ell_U(x).
\]
If $T=T_w$, then
\[
  \ell_{T_w}([r_u])=\frac{p(uw)}{p(u)},
\]
the probability of observing $w$ from predictive state $q_u$, which
is the reason for the name.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:faithful}
If $T,T'\in S_{\mathrm{lin}}$ have the same action on $\widehat X_p$ and
\[
  \ell_T(x)=\ell_{T'}(x)
  \qquad(x\in X_p),
\]
then $T=T'$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $u$ with $p(u)>0$.  If $Tr_u=0$, equality of the ray actions gives
$T'r_u=0$.  Otherwise the two outputs lie on the same positive ray.  Equality of
likelihoods says that they also have the same value under $\lambda$.  A nonzero
Hankel row has positive $\eps$-coordinate, so its positive ray together with that
coordinate determines it uniquely.  Hence $Tr_u=T'r_u$.

The nonzero rows $r_u$ span $V$, zero rows contribute nothing.  Therefore $T$ and
$T'$ agree on a spanning set and are equal.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The likelihood layer}
\label{subsec:likelihood}

Proposition~\ref{prop:faithful} says an operator is determined by its
state update together with its likelihood function.  This subsection
packages that as a structure theorem: the operator monoid embeds in a
monoid built from exactly those two ingredients, the update skeleton
$S_p$ and the likelihoods, and the quotient
$S_p\cong S_{\mathrm{lin}}/\kappa_{\mathrm{pred}}$ is then literally a
coordinate projection.

Let
\[
  \mathcal N=(\R_{\ge0})^{\widehat X_p},
\]
the nonnegative functions on $\widehat X_p$, a commutative monoid under
pointwise multiplication with identity the constant function
$\mathbf 1$.  For $t\in S_p$, precomposition $f\mapsto f\circ t$
respects pointwise products and fixes $\mathbf 1$.

\begin{definition}
Let $\mathcal N\rtimes S_p$ be the set $\mathcal N\times S_p$ with
multiplication
\[
  (f,s)(g,t)=\bigl((f\circ t)\,g,\ st\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}
$\mathcal N\rtimes S_p$ is a monoid with identity $(\mathbf 1,\id)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both bracketings of a triple product have second coordinate $stu$ and
first coordinate $(f\circ tu)(g\circ u)\,h$, using
$(f\circ t)\circ u=f\circ(tu)$ and that precomposition respects
pointwise products.  The identity checks are immediate.
\end{proof}

The order in the first coordinate is forced by the column convention:
the right factor's update acts first, so the left factor's weight must
be evaluated after it.

\begin{theorem}[Likelihood--skeleton embedding]
\label{thm:semidirect}
The map
\[
  \Lambda:S_{\mathrm{lin}}\longrightarrow\mathcal N\rtimes S_p,
  \qquad
  \Lambda(T)=(\ell_T,\alpha(T)),
\]
is an injective monoid homomorphism.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The cocycle identity $\ell_{TU}(x)=\ell_T(U\cdot x)\,\ell_U(x)$ holds
for every $x\in\widehat X_p$: at live states with $Ur_u\ne0$ it was
verified in the text, if $U$ kills $x$ both sides vanish, and at
$\dagger$ both sides equal $1$ (this is the reason for the convention
$\ell_T(\dagger)=1$).  It says exactly that first coordinates multiply
as in the definition, and second coordinates multiply because $\alpha$
is a homomorphism.  Injectivity is
Proposition~\ref{prop:faithful}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The interval reads only the second coordinate]
\label{cor:projection}
Let $\pi:\mathcal N\rtimes S_p\to S_p$ be the projection.  Then
$\alpha=\pi\circ\Lambda$, so $\kappa_{\mathrm{pred}}$ is the pullback of
$\ker\pi$: two operators are $\kappa_{\mathrm{pred}}$-related exactly
when their images under $\Lambda$ agree in the second coordinate.  Any
invariant computed from $S_p$, including the congruence interval of
Corollary~\ref{cor:con-inheritance}, is therefore blind to the
likelihood coordinate, the first component $\ell_T$ of $\Lambda(T)$,
by construction.
Section~\ref{subsec:computed-support} computes two processes where the
blindness is total: there $S_p$ carries no information beyond the
possibility layer.
\end{corollary}

The fibre of $\Lambda(S_{\mathrm{lin}})$ over $s\in S_p$, the set of
image elements with second coordinate $s$, consists of the likelihood
functions realised by operators with update $s$.
Section~\ref{sec:reset} computes a two element witness in one fibre:
$T_2$ and $T_{12}$ project to the same reset with distinct likelihood
functions.\footnote{For an i.i.d.\ process with all symbol
probabilities positive, $S_p$ is trivial and all content sits in a
single fibre: the opposite extreme.}

Sections~\ref{sec:agent}, \ref{sec:support}, and this one now assemble
into a tower, drawn in Figure~\ref{fig:tower}.  A process forces structure at three
separable levels, what is possible, what is conditionally expected,
and how likely each transition is, and the congruence interval reads
exactly the middle level (Corollary~\ref{cor:projection}), nothing here
decomposes $S_p$ itself in the cascade sense \cite{DrosteEtAl2011}.

\section{The reset chain: all three layers in closed form}
\label{sec:reset}

This section computes every layer of the tower for the simplest
nontrivial family of processes: full support first order Markov
chains. The possibility layer is
trivial (Remark~\ref{rem:full-support}), the update skeleton is a small
reset monoid whose congruence lattice is nevertheless non-distributive,
and the entire quantitative content, the actual probability values as
opposed to the update pattern, sits in the likelihood fibre.  In
particular, non distributivity of the inherited interval coexists with
a predictor that only stores the last symbol, so it cannot by itself
certify that internal features are entangled.

Let $\Obs=\{1,2,3\}$ and consider the stationary first-order Markov chain with
uniform stationary distribution (the matrix below is doubly stochastic)
and transition matrix
\[
  M=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \frac12&\frac13&\frac16\\[2pt]
    \frac13&\frac16&\frac12\\[2pt]
    \frac16&\frac12&\frac13
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The matrix is entrywise positive, and its three rows are distinct.  After
any nonempty history, the conditional future
law depend only on the last observed symbol, write $g_i$ for the
predictive state after observing $i$, its next symbol distribution is
row $i$ of $M$.  The chain is started in its stationary distribution,
(uniform because $M$ is doubly stochastic), so before the first
observation the initial predictive state $q_\eps$ assigns each symbol
probability $\tfrac13$.  No row of $M$ is uniform, so $q_\eps$ differs
from every $g_i$ already on the next symbol, and
\[
  X_p=\{q_\eps,g_1,g_2,g_3\}.
\]

Observing $i$ sends every state in $X_p$ to $g_i$ and fixes $\dagger$.  Let
$c_i$ denote this reset map.  The canonical predictive transition monoid is
\[
  R_3^1=\{1,c_1,c_2,c_3\},
  \qquad c_i c_j=c_i,
\]
where the displayed multiplication uses ordinary composition, with the left
factor applied last.  Figure~\ref{fig:reset} draws the action.

This example also makes
$\theta_{\mathrm{scal}}\subsetneq\kappa_{\mathrm{pred}}$ concrete.  The
restrictions of $g_1,g_2,g_3$ to one symbol futures are the rows of $M$, and
$\det M=-1/12$, so these states are linearly independent.  In that basis,
\[
  T_2g_i=M_{i2}g_2,
  \qquad
  T_{12}g_i=M_{i1}M_{12}g_2.
\]
The coefficient vectors are
\[
  \left(\frac13,\frac16,\frac12\right)
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \left(\frac16,\frac19,\frac1{18}\right),
\]
which are not proportional.  Hence $T_2$ and $T_{12}$ are not scalar
multiples, although both reset every state in $X_p$ to $g_2$ and fix
$\dagger$.
In the language of Section~\ref{subsec:likelihood}, this shows two
distinct elements of the fibre of the likelihood extension over the
single reset $c_2$.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:reset-con}
For $n\ge1$, let
\[
  R_n^1=\{1,c_1,\ldots,c_n\},
  \qquad c_i c_j=c_i,
\]
with $1$ an identity.  Every non-universal congruence has $\{1\}$ as a singleton
class and restricts to an arbitrary equivalence relation on
$\{c_1,\ldots,c_n\}$.  Consequently,
\[
  \Con(R_n^1)\cong \Pi_n\oplus\mathbf 1,
\]
the partition lattice on $n$ points with one new top element adjoined.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First take any partition of the constants and keep $1$ in a single class.
It is a congruence.  Left multiplication of equivalent constants by $1$ preserves
their equivalence, left multiplication by a constant makes both products equal,
and the analogous right-multiplication checks follow from $c_ic_j=c_i$.

Conversely, suppose a congruence identifies $1$ with some $c_i$.  For every $j$,
right multiplication by $c_j$ gives
\[
  c_j=1c_j\equiv c_ic_j=c_i.
\]
Thus all constants are equivalent to $c_i$, and hence to $1$, the congruence is
universal.  Every non-universal congruence therefore fixes $1$, after which its
restriction to the constants is an arbitrary partition.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
For the three-symbol Markov chain above, $\Con(S_p)$ contains
\[
  \Pi_3\cong M_3
\]
as the interval below the congruence that merges all three reset maps while
leaving the identity separate.  In particular, $\Con(S_p)$ is
non distributive.


\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:markov-reset-general}
Let $p$ be a first order Markov chain on $n\ge2$ observation symbols whose
initial distribution is strictly positive and whose transition matrix is strictly positive and has pairwise distinct rows.  Then
\[
  S_p\cong R_n^1.
\]
Consequently,
\[ 
  \Con(S_p)\cong\Pi_n\oplus\mathbf 1,
\]
and it is non-distributive for every $n\ge3$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
After every nonempty history, the conditional future law is the law $g_i$
associated with its last symbol $i$.  Distinct transition rows make the $g_i$
pairwise distinct.  Full support of both the initial distribution and the
transition matrix ensures that observing $i$ is possible from every live
predictive state and resets that state to $g_i$.  Thus the generated
transformations are precisely the identity and the $n$ distinct reset maps
$c_i$, which satisfy $c_ic_j=c_i$.  The congruence statement follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:reset-con}.
\end{proof}

An exact predictor for this process only needs to retain the latest symbol (and
an initial state before the first observation).  Its update rule is a reset, and
its predictions are the corresponding row of $M$ followed by the same Markov
law.  This representation is finite,
explicit, and transparent, Section~\ref{sec:rank} makes the word
transparent precise, as the statement that every generator has live
rank one, so the process licenses total forgetting at every step.
Nevertheless its canonical transition monoid has an $M_3$ congruence
interval.

Combining this example with Corollary~\ref{cor:con-inheritance} gives two
conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item every reachable exact predictor of this process inherits a
        non distributive congruence interval,
  \item that non distributivity cannot itself imply that the predictor uses
        entangled, distributed, or uninterpretable features,
        Corollary~\ref{cor:projection} gives the mechanism, the witness
        lives in a projection that discards likelihoods, and here the
        likelihoods are the whole content.
\end{enumerate}


\section{What distributivity does say}
\label{sec:coordinates}

In a finite
lattice $L$, an element $j\ne\bot$ is join irreducible when
$j=a\join b$ forces $j=a$ or $j=b$.  Every element of a finite lattice
is the join of the join irreducibles below it, so the assignment
\[
  x\longmapsto\J(x)=\{j\text{ join-irreducible}:j\le x\}
\]
is injective for \emph{every} finite lattice, not only a distributive
one.  Birkhoff's representation theorem for finite lattices says that
$L$ is distributive exactly when this assignment is a lattice
isomorphism onto the down-sets of the join-irreducibles (subsets
closed downwards, ordered by inclusion), equivalently, exactly when
$L$ contains neither $M_3$ nor $N_5$ of Figure~\ref{fig:m3n5} as a
sublattice \cite{DaveyPriestley2002}.

Reading $\J(x)$ as a bit-vector, the indicator of which
join-irreducibles lie below $x$, what fails without distributivity is
therefore not injectivity but the union rule
\[
  \J(a\join b)=\J(a)\cup\J(b).
\]
In $M_3$ the five elements still have five distinct bit-vectors, but
joining two atoms activates the third: the canonical coordinates are
not union-compositional, equivalently, there is no union/intersection
representation of the lattice.  This is the precise content of the
word obstruction as used in this paper: $M_3$ and $N_5$ block a
compositional coordinate representation of the lattice of admissible
lumpings, and nothing more is claimed for them.

Two cautions bound the reading from the other side.  Even
$\Con(S)\cong2^n$, the lattice of all subsets of an $n$-element set,
does not by itself prove that $S$ decomposes into independent
subsystems: a direct product decomposition requires complementary
factor congruences that permute,
$\alpha\circ\beta=\beta\circ\alpha$, where $\circ$ is composition of
relations, $a\,(\alpha\circ\beta)\,c$ meaning
$a\,\alpha\,b\,\beta\,c$ for some $b$
\cite[\S II.7]{BurrisSankappanavar1981}.  And the finite theorem is
applied in this paper only to explicitly finite monoids or explicitly
finite quotient intervals.

\section{How much must be remembered}
\label{sec:rank}

The congruence interval is a single global invariant.  This section
extracts a word by word one: for each word $w$, a lower bound on the number
of distinctions any exact predictor must still be holding after reading
$w$.

\begin{definition}[Live rank]
For $s\in S_p$, the live rank of $s$ is the number of live states in its
image,
\[
  \rk(s)=\bigl|\,s(\widehat X_p)\cap X_p\,\bigr|
  \in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}.
\]
\end{definition}

Composition cannot increase live rank on either side.  For $s\circ t$,
the image of the composite is contained in the image of $s$, so
$\rk(s\circ t)\le\rk(s)$, and the image of $s\circ t$ is
$s(\mathrm{im}\,t)$, whose live part is contained in the image under $s$
of the live part of $\mathrm{im}\,t$ (since $s(\dagger)=\dagger$ is not
live), so $\rk(s\circ t)\le\rk(t)$ as well.

For a word $w$, the live states in the image of $\tau_w$ are exactly the
predictive states $q_{uw}$ over histories $u$ with $p(uw)>0$.  So
$\rk(\tau_w)$ counts the distinct conditional future laws that can hold
immediately after an occurrence of $w$.

\begin{theorem}[Inherited memory bound]
\label{thm:rank-bound}
Let $A$ be a reachable exact predictive agent for $p$.  For every word
$w\in\Obs^*$,
\[
  \rk(\tau_w)\ \le\
  \bigl|\,\delta_w(Q)\cap Q^+\,\bigr|.
\]
The right-hand side is the number of distinct live agent states
reachable by a history ending in $w$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:predictor-quotient},
$L\circ\delta_w=\tau_w\circ L$ with $L$ surjective, so
$\tau_w(\widehat X_p)=L(\delta_w(Q))$.  A live element of the left-hand
side is the image under $L$ of some element of $\delta_w(Q)$, and that
element must lie in $Q^+$ because $L(d)=\dagger$.  So $L$ maps
$\delta_w(Q)\cap Q^+$ onto $\tau_w(\widehat X_p)\cap X_p$, giving the
inequality.  For the interpretation: $Q=\{\delta_u(q_0):p(u)>0\}
\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{d\}$ by reachability, so
$\delta_w(Q)\cap Q^+=\{\delta_{uw}(q_0):p(uw)>0\}\cap Q^+$, the live
states reachable by histories with final block $w$.
\end{proof}

The bound is determined by the process alone and holds word by word for
every exact predictor, whatever its implementation.  Its two extremes
have names and appear as the two poles of this paper's examples.

\begin{example}[Total forgetting: the reset chain]
Call $w$ synchronising when $\rk(\tau_w)=1$: after reading $w$, a single
conditional future law holds, regardless of what came before, so an
exact predictor is permitted to forget everything preceding $w$.  In the
Markov chains of Proposition~\ref{prop:markov-reset-general} every
single symbol is synchronising: $\rk(c_i)=1$ for each reset map, and
only the identity has full live rank.  This is the precise content of
calling the last-symbol predictor transparent.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[No forgetting: the P\'olya urn]
\label{ex:polya}
Let $\Obs=\{\mathsf r,\mathsf b\}$ and let $p$ be the P\'olya urn started
from one red and one blue ball: each symbol is drawn with probability
proportional to the current count of its colour, and the drawn colour's
count then increases by one.  Explicitly, any word with $r$ reds and $b$
blues has
\[
  p(w)=\frac{r!\,b!}{(r+b+1)!}>0,
\]
so the process has full support and the order of symbols does not
matter.  The predictive state after a history is therefore a function of
the pair $(r,b)$ alone.  Distinct pairs give distinct states, by two
finite-future probabilities: with $n=r+b+2$,
\[
  q_{(r,b)}(\mathsf r)=\frac{r+1}{n},
  \qquad
  q_{(r,b)}(\mathsf r\mathsf r)=\frac{(r+1)(r+2)}{n(n+1)},
\]
and these two values determine first $n$ and then $r$.  One-step
probabilities alone do not suffice: $(1,1)$ and $(2,2)$ both predict
red with probability $\tfrac12$, but their two-step predictions
differ, $\tfrac{3}{10}$ against $\tfrac27$.

The updates act on states by
$(r,b)\mapsto(r+1,b)$ and $(r,b)\mapsto(r,b+1)$, so every $\tau_w$ is
injective (Figure~\ref{fig:urn}) and
\[
  \rk(\tau_w)=\infty
  \qquad(w\in\Obs^*).
\]
\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering

    \foreach \b in {0,1,2,3}

\label{fig:urn}

No word is synchronising, and by
Theorem~\ref{thm:rank-bound} no exact predictor of this process can
bound its number of states: it must hold unboundedly many distinctions
forever.  Note that $X_p$ is infinite here, so the Hankel rank of
Section~\ref{sec:linear} is infinite as well, nothing in this section
assumed finiteness.
\end{example}

The reset chain and the urn are opposite answers to the same question.
In one, the world licenses total forgetting at every step and the forced
memory is a single symbol, in the other, the world licenses no merging
at all and the forced memory grows without bound.  The quantity
$\rk(\tau_w)$ interpolates between them word by word.\footnote{The sets $I_k=\{s\in S_p:\rk(s)\le k\}$ are two-sided ideals, so the rank profile also stratifies
$S_p$ into Rees quotients, each again contributing an inherited
interval via congruence correspondence, we do not pursue this here.}

Synchronisation is a studied notion on both sides of this construction.
For $\varepsilon$-machines, Travers and Crutchfield analyse when and
how fast an observer of a stationary finite-state source pins down the
predictive state, including a polynomial-time test for the existence of
a synchronising word
\cite{TraversCrutchfield2011a,TraversCrutchfield2011b}, for
deterministic automata, reset words are the subject of a large
literature around the \v{C}ern\'y conjecture \cite{Volkov2008}.  The
contribution of this section is neither notion but the transport:
Theorem~\ref{thm:rank-bound} carries the full rank profile, not only
the synchronising case, to every exact predictor of the process, with
no stationarity or finiteness hypothesis.


\section{The controlled setting}
\label{sec:controllers}

Actions require a controlled version of the predictive machine.  Fix finite
action and output alphabets $A$ and $O$.  An anticipation-free interface
$\mathcal I$ (anticipation-free: outputs up to time $n$ depend only on
actions up to time $n$, which is what the display below enforces)
specifies probabilities
\[
  \mathcal I(o_{1:n}\mid a_{1:n})
\]
which are normalised and causally prefix consistent:
\[
  \mathcal I(\eps\mid\eps)=1,
  \qquad
  \sum_{o_n\in O}\mathcal I(o_{1:n}\mid a_{1:n})
  =\mathcal I(o_{1:n-1}\mid a_{1:n-1})
  \quad(n\ge1).
\]
A history
\[
  h=(a_1,o_1)\cdots(a_n,o_n)
\]
is admissible when $\mathcal I(o_{1:n}\mid a_{1:n})>0$.  Its residual
interface is
\[
  \mathcal I^h(v\mid u)
  =
  \frac{\mathcal I(o_{1:n}v\mid a_{1:n}u)}
       {\mathcal I(o_{1:n}\mid a_{1:n})},
\]
for equally long future action and output words $u$ and $v$.  Define
\[
  X_{\mathcal I}=\{\mathcal I^h:h\text{ admissible}\},
  \qquad
  \widehat X_{\mathcal I}
  =X_{\mathcal I}\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{\dagger\}.
\]
Appending an action-output pair gives the total canonical update
\[
  \tau_{a,o}(\mathcal I^h)=
  \begin{cases}
    \mathcal I^{h(a,o)},&\mathcal I^h(o\mid a)>0,\\
    \dagger,&\mathcal I^h(o\mid a)=0,
  \end{cases}
  \qquad
  \tau_{a,o}(\dagger)=\dagger.
\]
The map is well-defined: if $\mathcal I^h=\mathcal I^{h'}$, then
$\mathcal I^h(o\mid a)=\mathcal I^{h'}(o\mid a)$, and whenever this probability
is positive the updated residuals $\mathcal I^{h(a,o)}$ and
$\mathcal I^{h'(a,o)}$ coincide.  The controlled predictive transition monoid is
\[
  S_{\mathcal I}=\langle\tau_{a,o}:(a,o)\in A\times O\rangle.
\]
This is the $\varepsilon$-transducer construction for controlled processes
\cite{BarnettCrutchfield2015}.

\begin{remark}[Relation to partially observable cMDPs]
Every partially observable cMDP together with a fixed initial law induces an
anticipation-free interface, by letting $\mathcal I(o_{1:n}\mid a_{1:n})$ be the
probability of the output word under the action word.  Many latent models induce
the same interface, and $X_{\mathcal I}$ is a quotient of the belief simplex:
belief determines the residual interface but not conversely.  The monoid and
congruence conclusions below depend only on $\mathcal I$, so they are invariant
under change of latent realisation and apply equally to interfaces admitting no
finite POMDP realisation.  Conversely, recovering the latent kernel $P$ alone
does not determine $\mathcal I$ without the observation kernel and initial law.
\end{remark}

A goal is a measurable set of action--output streams.  A controller $C$ together
with $\mathcal I$ and a goal $g$ induces a distribution over streams, write
$\Pr(g\mid C,\mathcal I)$ for the resulting achievement probability.  For
$0\le\delta<1$, $C$ is $\delta$-optimal on a goal set $\mathcal G$ when
\[
  \Pr(g\mid C,\mathcal I)\ \ge\ (1-\delta)\,\sup_{C'}\Pr(g\mid C',\mathcal I)
  \qquad(g\in\mathcal G),
\]
matching the relative-failure criterion of \cite{Richens2025,Cifuentes2026}.

\subsection{Two meanings of `the controller contains a model'}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item Behavioural containment: the environment's transition law or
        residual interface can be recovered from the controller's policy.
  \item Internal quotient: there is a surjective map from
        reachable controller states to $X_{\mathcal I}$ which commutes with
        every action-output update.
\end{enumerate}
The second statement implies a quotient of transition monoids.  The first is
an identifiability statement about behaviour and does not on its own locate a
model in the controller's activations or recurrent state.  For behavioural
containment, the recovery map must be fixed uniformly over a class of
environments sharing the same action and output alphabets and the same goal
set, allowing it to depend on the unknown environment would make the claim
vacuous.

\subsection{Why optimality alone is insufficient}
\label{subsec:controller-no-go}

The proof uses the controller conventions of
Subsection~\ref{subsec:controller-formalism}.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:controller-no-go}
Optimality on a fixed goal set does not force either form of
containment.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take $\mathcal G=\{g\}$ with $g$ the set of all streams: every controller
achieves $g$ with probability one, so every controller is $0$-optimal in every
environment.  In particular, take an interface with at least two distinct
predictive states and a controller with one live state (and the dead state)
which chooses one fixed action after every history.  A dead-state-preserving
map from this controller cannot surject onto two or more live predictive
states, and the same constant policy is optimal for a family of environments
with common action and output alphabets but different transition laws.  No
recovery map fixed across that family can recover the environment from
the policy.
\end{proof}

The example is not an artefact of a trivial goal.  Let $g$ instead be
``eventually take action $a$'', which is achievable and non-constant.  Its
unique optimal policy takes $a$ immediately, is memoryless, and is identical
across every interface on the same alphabets, so neither containment follows.
Richens et al.\ prove a stronger negative result: even a policy optimal
for every depth-one goal identifies no nontrivial bound on the
transition probabilities \cite{Richens2025}, a fortiori the same holds
under partial observation.

\subsection{Behavioural recovery}

What optimality on a single goal cannot do, near-optimality across a
sufficiently deep goal family can.  Richens et al.\ show, for finite, communicating (every state reachable
from every other), fully observed controlled Markov processes with deterministic
goal-conditioned policies, that $\delta$-optimality on all goals of depth at
most $n$ determines an estimate of the transition kernel whose entrywise
error vanishes as $n$ grows, for any fixed $\delta<1$ \cite{Richens2025}.
Cifuentes extends this behavioural recovery to stochastic policies and to
observation-based policies in partially observable cMDPs, and separately
reduces the goal family to width two for deterministic policies at a
constant-factor cost in depth, the stochastic argument requires
$\delta<\tfrac12$ because its recovery goals are two-alternative bets,
and a
$\tfrac12$-optimal agent may split both branches evenly and reveal nothing,
the width-two argument also assumes $\delta<\tfrac12$, but there only as
a proof simplification
\cite{Cifuentes2026}.  In every case the recovery map is from the policy, not
from the policy's implementation, so a further bridge is needed for an
internal quotient.

There is also a scope restriction to keep in view.  In the partially
observable extension, goals are still formulated and evaluated on latent
environment states, even though the policy observes only outputs, the proof
works because the querying policy ignores observations, so the observation
kernel drops out of the calculation.  Observation-phrased goals are left open
in \cite{Cifuentes2026}.  The interface formalism above removes both
restrictions, which is exactly the gap between that setting and the one used
here.

\subsection{The conditional internal quotient}
\label{subsec:controller-formalism}

The bridge below is conditional on a state-local hypothesis.  Whether
Richens--Cifuentes behavioural recovery yields that hypothesis in a suitable
exact limit is open, until it is settled,
Theorem~\ref{thm:controller-bridge} and Corollary~\ref{cor:controller-con}
should be read as conditional results.

Fix a goal set $\mathcal G$.  A recurrent goal-conditioned controller
(recurrent meaning it carries an internal state updated at every step, not
recurrence in the Markov-chain sense)
\[
  C=(Q,q_0,d,\{\delta_{a,o}\}_{(a,o)\in A\times O},\pi)
\]
has the same components as the predictive agents of
Section~\ref{sec:agent}, with the alphabet $A\times O$ in place of $\Obs$ and
one addition: a deterministic goal-conditioned policy
$\pi:Q\times\mathcal G\to A$.  For a history
$h=(a_1,o_1)\cdots(a_n,o_n)$, write
\[
  \delta_h
  =\delta_{a_n,o_n}\circ\cdots\circ\delta_{a_1,o_1},
  \qquad q_h=\delta_h(q_0),
\]
with $Q^+=\{q_h:h\text{ admissible}\}$ and
$Q=Q^+\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{d\}$.

The update maps are goal-independent and total, satisfy
$\delta_{a,o}(d)=d$, and send $q_h$ to $d$ whenever $h(a,o)$ is
impossible.  They are defined for every counterfactual intervention history,
not only histories generated by the controller's on-policy actions.  If goal
progress affects recurrence, that memory must be included explicitly in $Q$.

For $q\in Q$, define its total continuation-policy signature by
\[
  B_C(q)(g,k)=\pi(\delta_k(q),g),
  \qquad
  g\in\mathcal G,\quad k\in(A\times O)^*.
\]
Using all counterfactual continuations, including those sent to $d$, makes
$B_C(q)$ a function of the controller state alone rather than of the history
chosen to represent it.

\begin{definition}[State-local behavioural recovery]
A recovery map $\mathcal R$ is state-local when it is fixed uniformly
over the environment class under consideration and, for every admissible
history $h$,
\[
  \mathcal R(B_C(q_h))=\mathcal I^h.
\]
\end{definition}

This condition says that the policy information available from the recurrent
state determines the residual controlled process.  It is stronger than global
recovery from a policy oracle, because it requires the recoverable information
to factor through the controller state.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:controller-bridge}
Suppose a reachable recurrent goal-conditioned controller admits an exact
state-local recovery map.  Then there is a surjection
\[
  L:Q\rightarrow\widehat X_{\mathcal I}
\]
satisfying
\[
  L\circ\delta_{a,o}=\tau_{a,o}\circ L
  \qquad((a,o)\in A\times O).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a state reached by an admissible history $h$, define
\[
  L(q_h)=\mathcal R(B_C(q_h))=\mathcal I^h,
\]
and send the controller's dead state to the predictive dead state.  This is
well-defined because equal controller states have the same complete
continuation-policy signature and hence the same recovered residual interface.
It is surjective because every element of $X_{\mathcal I}$ has an admissible
history representative, while the controller dead state is sent to
$\dagger$.

If $h(a,o)$ is admissible, state-local recovery at the successor gives
\[
  L(\delta_{a,o}q_h)
  =\mathcal I^{h(a,o)}
  =\tau_{a,o}(\mathcal I^h)
  =\tau_{a,o}L(q_h).
\]
For an impossible extension, both sides are the absorbing dead state.
At $d$ the equation holds because both $d$ and $\dagger$ are absorbing.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:controller-con}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:controller-bridge},
Lemma~\ref{lem:descent} applied to $L$ gives a surjective monoid
homomorphism $\Phi_C:S_C\rightarrow S_{\mathcal I}$, where
$S_C=\langle\delta_{a,o}:(a,o)\in A\times O\rangle$, and, with
$\kappa_C=\ker\Phi_C$,
\[
  \Con(S_{\mathcal I})
  \cong[\kappa_C,\top]
  \subseteq\Con(S_C).
\]
Thus every $M_3$ or $N_5$ obstruction in the canonical controlled predictive
monoid is inherited by the recurrent controller.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[The tower and the rank profile transport]
\label{rem:controlled-transport}
Everything in Sections~\ref{sec:support} and~\ref{sec:rank} used only
Lemma~\ref{lem:descent} and the absorbing-$\dagger$ conventions, so it
applies verbatim over the alphabet $A\times O$.  The controlled process has
its own possibility layer, built from the follower sets of admissible
action--output histories, and its own live rank and ideal chain on
$S_{\mathcal I}$.  Under the hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:controller-bridge}, a controller therefore inherits the
same nested intervals and the same word by word memory bounds as an exact
predictor.  We make no claim about the likelihood layer here: that would
require a controlled linear realisation, which we have not set up.
\end{remark}

The hypotheses above are deliberately exact, and two open problems remain.
First, state-locality: whether the exact limit of behavioural recovery
factors through a recurrent controller state, rather than through the policy
as a whole.  Second, robustness: the published results bound the error of a
recovered kernel, which is behavioural approximation, monoid descent would
instead need approximate intertwining, a bound of the form
$L\circ\delta_{a,o}\approx\tau_{a,o}\circ L$ on residual interfaces, and the
first does not yield the second, in part because a bound on the latent
kernel $P$ leaves the observation kernel and initial law unrecovered.
Supplying approximate intertwining, and with it the persistence of finite
$M_3/N_5$ witnesses under controlled approximation, remains open.


\section{Results and limitations}
\label{sec:results}

The theorem chain is short: conditional future laws form a canonical
machine, every reachable exact predictor surjects onto it, descent
turns that surjection into an inherited congruence interval, the same
mechanism yields the outer possibility interval and, in the linear
case, the likelihood extension whose projection the interval reads,
and the rank profile bounds forced memory word by word.  The reset
chains and the computed examples bracket where the inherited structure
can live: entirely in the likelihoods, or entirely in bare
possibility.

Three limits should be read alongside.  First, nothing here locates
features in a network's activations: the results constrain quotients
of update dynamics, not representations.  Second, exactness is a
strong hypothesis, the approximate version, approximate
intertwining and the persistence of finite witnesses, is open, and
the controller results are conditional on state-locality
(Section~\ref{sec:controllers}).  Third, the individual layers are
close to known territory, causal states, follower-set automata,
synchronisation theory, the projective/likelihood split, and the
claims made here are the transport of that structure to arbitrary
exact predictors and the stratification itself, not the layer objects.

Three problems are left open.  Whether an impossibility-built witness
always descends to the possibility layer, testable only in the regime
$\bot\subsetneq\kappa_{\mathrm{supp}}\subsetneq\top$
(Section~\ref{subsec:computed-support}).  A criterion separating
obstructions that block genuine decompositions of the monoid itself
from those that are counting artefacts, congruence permutability is
the natural candidate (Section~\ref{sec:coordinates}).  And, for controllers,
state-locality and approximate intertwining
(Section~\ref{sec:controllers}).
